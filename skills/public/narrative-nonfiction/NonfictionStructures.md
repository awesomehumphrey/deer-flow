# Non-Fiction Structures Reference

Structural frameworks for mapping narrative non-fiction at both the book level and the chapter level. These complement the Intellectual Journey Framework with mechanical scaffolding.

## Book-Level Structures

### The Investigation Arc

The most common structure for narrative non-fiction dealing with systemic problems.

| Stage | % | Purpose | Layer Impact |
|-------|---|---------|-------------|
| **Opening scene** | 0-5% | A concrete person or moment that embodies the problem | Landscape, Relationships |
| **The question** | 5-10% | What made the author start looking? | Intellectual Journey, Mystery |
| **Context** | 10-20% | Historical and institutional background | Landscape, Meaning |
| **First evidence** | 20-35% | Initial findings that confirm the problem is real | Narrative Thread, Mystery |
| **Complications** | 35-50% | Evidence that makes the picture more complex than expected | Intellectual Journey, Meaning |
| **The human cost** | 50-65% | Stories of people affected; the problem made visceral | Relationships, Prose |
| **Full scope** | 65-80% | The systemic nature of the problem becomes undeniable | Meaning, Landscape |
| **Toward solutions** | 80-90% | What is being done; what could be done | Narrative Thread, Relationships |
| **Implications** | 90-100% | What this means for the reader; call to action or reflection | Meaning, Intellectual Journey |

**Best for:** Books about systemic problems (surveillance, inequality, institutional failure, values violations in software).

### The Discovery Narrative

Structured around the author's personal journey of understanding.

| Stage | % | Purpose | Layer Impact |
|-------|---|---------|-------------|
| **Encounter** | 0-10% | The author encounters something that does not make sense | Intellectual Journey, Mystery |
| **Initial exploration** | 10-25% | Early research, first interviews, preliminary findings | Narrative Thread, Landscape |
| **False understanding** | 25-35% | The author thinks they understand; the reader may agree | Mystery, Meaning |
| **Disruption** | 35-50% | Evidence that shatters the false understanding | Intellectual Journey, Mystery |
| **Deeper investigation** | 50-70% | Following the new thread; the real picture emerges | All layers active |
| **Synthesis** | 70-85% | The author arrives at a new understanding | Meaning, Intellectual Journey |
| **Implications** | 85-100% | What the discovery means for the field and the reader | Meaning, Relationships |

**Best for:** Books driven by the author's personal research journey (scientific discovery, academic investigation, personal-is-political).

---

### The Portrait

Structured around a person, institution, or system examined in depth.

| Stage | % | Purpose | Layer Impact |
|-------|---|---------|-------------|
| **First impression** | 0-10% | The subject as the world sees them | Landscape, Prose |
| **Surface examination** | 10-25% | What is publicly known; the official narrative | Narrative Thread, Mystery |
| **Cracks appear** | 25-40% | Evidence that contradicts the surface | Mystery, Intellectual Journey |
| **Deeper access** | 40-60% | Behind the scenes; primary sources, interviews | Relationships, Landscape |
| **The real picture** | 60-80% | The subject as they actually are | Meaning, Intellectual Journey |
| **Reckoning** | 80-100% | What the portrait reveals about the larger world | Meaning, Relationships |

**Best for:** Profiles of people, companies, or institutions (tech companies, regulatory bodies, individual researchers).

---

### The Thesis-Driven Structure

The author's argument is stated relatively early and the book marshals evidence to support and complicate it.

| Stage | % | Purpose | Layer Impact |
|-------|---|---------|-------------|
| **The claim** | 0-10% | State the thesis, usually through a concrete example | Meaning, Narrative Thread |
| **Evidence stream 1** | 10-30% | First body of evidence supporting the thesis | Narrative Thread, Landscape |
| **Complications** | 30-45% | Counter-arguments and edge cases | Intellectual Journey, Mystery |
| **Evidence stream 2** | 45-60% | Second body of evidence, from a different angle | Relationships, Landscape |
| **Synthesis** | 60-75% | How the evidence streams combine into a fuller picture | Meaning, Intellectual Journey |
| **Implications** | 75-90% | What this means for policy, practice, or the reader | Meaning, Relationships |
| **Closing argument** | 90-100% | The thesis restated with the weight of all evidence behind it | Meaning, Prose |

**Best for:** Books that advance a specific argument (AI alignment, values in software, policy proposals).

## Chapter-Level Structure

### The Five Commandments (adapted from Story Grid)

Every chapter in narrative non-fiction contains these five elements:

| Element | Non-Fiction Application |
|---------|----------------------|
| **Opening hook** | A scene, question, or statement that makes the reader commit to the chapter |
| **Progressive evidence** | Evidence and analysis that builds, each piece raising the stakes or complicating the picture |
| **Complication** | The moment where the evidence takes an unexpected turn, or the picture becomes more complex than anticipated |
| **Synthesis** | The chapter's key insight, arrived at through the evidence rather than stated in advance |
| **Forward hook** | A question, implication, or unresolved thread that pulls the reader into the next chapter |

### Chapter Archetypes

| Archetype | Structure | Best For |
|-----------|----------|----------|
| **The case study** | Person/situation, context, evidence, analysis, implication | Making abstract problems concrete |
| **The explainer** | Question, background, mechanism, evidence, "so what" | Making complex systems accessible |
| **The revelation** | Setup, investigation, false lead, real finding, implications | Building suspense and surprise |
| **The contrast** | Position A, evidence for A, Position B, evidence for B, synthesis | Exploring competing explanations |
| **The personal** | Author's experience, reflection, connection to larger theme | Building author-reader intimacy |
| **The panorama** | Multiple simultaneous perspectives on one event or system | Showing systemic scope |

## Transitions Between Chapters

The connective tissue between chapters is as important as the chapters themselves.

**Strong transitions:**
- End Chapter N with a question; open Chapter N+1 with a scene that addresses it
- End Chapter N with a person; open Chapter N+1 with the same person in a new context
- End Chapter N with an implication; open Chapter N+1 with evidence that tests it
- End Chapter N with a specific detail; open Chapter N+1 with the same detail from a different angle

**Weak transitions:**
- "In this chapter, we will examine..." (textbook voice)
- "Now let us turn to..." (lecture voice)
- No connection at all; chapters read as independent essays
- Chronological sequencing without causal logic ("after that, I looked at...")

## Promises, Progress, Payoff (adapted from Sanderson)

| Element | Non-Fiction Application |
|---------|----------------------|
| **Promise** | The book's opening chapters create expectations about what the reader will learn and how they will feel |
| **Progress** | Each chapter should visibly advance at least one promise |
| **Payoff** | Every question raised must be answered; every thread introduced must be resolved or explicitly left open |

**The Promise Contract:** Every promise made to the reader (explicitly or implicitly) must be paid off. Unfulfilled promises feel like sloppy research. Fulfilled promises the reader forgot about feel like masterful structure.
