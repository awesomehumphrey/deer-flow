# Intellectual Journey Framework

Adapted from Will Storr's *The Science of Storytelling*. In fiction, Storr's framework centres on the "sacred flaw" and character transformation. In narrative non-fiction, the equivalent engine is the **research arc**: the author begins with assumptions that are gradually disrupted by evidence, leading to a revised and deeper understanding.

## The Brain as Story Processor

Storr's foundational insight applies equally to non-fiction: **the brain is a story processor, not a logic processor.** Readers do not absorb arguments through logic alone. They follow narratives.

Key principles:
- The brain constructs reality by observing cause-and-effect and building theories
- A book is "a portal, a hallucination within the hallucination"
- Non-fiction that presents evidence as a journey of discovery feels real to the brain's prediction engine
- Non-fiction that presents evidence as a static catalogue feels artificial

**Application:** Write research as a process of discovery. Let the reader follow the trail. Do not front-load conclusions.

---

## Unexpected Change

The brain is wired as a change-detection machine. When the intellectual landscape is stable, the brain relaxes. When something changes unexpectedly, attention spikes.

**Application in non-fiction:**
- **Opening:** Establish a conventional understanding, then disrupt it with evidence
- **Throughout:** Every chapter should contain at least one moment where the evidence surprises
- **The disruption hierarchy:** Small surprises (within a section) build to medium surprises (chapter-level revelations) build to the book's central argument (the thing the reader did not expect to believe by the end)
- **Disruption types:** Evidential (new data contradicts assumption), conceptual (a familiar idea is reframed), personal (the author's own experience challenges received wisdom), structural (an institution behaves in unexpected ways)

## The Initial Assumption (replacing the Sacred Flaw)

In fiction, the protagonist's "sacred flaw" is a misbelief they cling to. In non-fiction, the equivalent is the **initial assumption**: the conventional wisdom, the common understanding, or the author's own starting position that the book's evidence will progressively dismantle.

### The Three-Level Research Arc

The initial assumption creates productive tension across three levels:

| Level | Domain | What It Is | Example |
|-------|--------|-----------|---------|
| **Surface Question** | Narrative Thread | The question the reader consciously follows | "Why do apps deceive users?" |
| **Deeper Pattern** | Intellectual Journey | What the evidence actually reveals | Structural incentives make deception rational |
| **Thesis** | Meaning | The universal insight the journey illuminates | Software values alignment is a distinct, urgent problem |

### Building an Initial Assumption

1. **Conventional wisdom:** What does the general reader believe about this subject before reading?
2. **Author's starting point:** What did the author believe before the research? (This may differ from conventional wisdom.)
3. **First complications:** What early evidence began to undermine the assumption?
4. **Progressive disruption:** How did accumulating evidence reshape understanding?
5. **The assumption as identity:** Why do people (including the author) resist revising this belief? What makes it comfortable?

### The Question/Evidence Inversion

The deeper pattern is often the **inverse** of the initial assumption:
- Assumption: "Dark patterns are the work of bad actors" > Finding: "Dark patterns are a structural feature of the attention economy"
- Assumption: "Users accept terms because they agree" > Finding: "Consent architecture is designed to prevent meaningful choice"
- Assumption: "AI alignment is the urgent problem" > Finding: "Software values alignment is already here, already harmful, already at scale"

### Crisis and Synthesis

The book's climactic chapters force the full scope of the problem into view:
- The evidence can no longer be contained within the initial assumption
- The author must synthesise a new understanding
- **Positive arc:** The author arrives at a thesis that reframes the field (most non-fiction)
- **Tragic arc:** The author demonstrates that no adequate solution exists yet (rare but powerful)
- **Advocacy arc:** The author proposes concrete changes based on the synthesis

## Cause and Effect

Non-fiction argument is NOT "and then... and then... and then." It is "because of this... this was discovered... which revealed..."

**Storr's principle applied:** The brain trusts causal chains. Non-fiction that shows clear evidential chains feels rigorous. Non-fiction that presents findings in isolation feels like a catalogue.

**Application:**
- Every chapter should have a clear causal link to the previous one
- Research decisions must have logical justification ("I investigated X because the evidence from Chapter 3 pointed there")
- Show the process of elimination, not just the conclusion
- Let the reader see dead ends and revised hypotheses; these build trust

---

## Status and Credibility

In fiction, characters negotiate social status. In non-fiction, the author negotiates **credibility** with the reader.

**Credibility in non-fiction:**
- Showing your working builds trust (transparency about method)
- Acknowledging limitations and counter-arguments raises status
- Admitting when you were wrong or surprised raises status
- Overclaiming or hiding uncertainty destroys credibility
- The strongest position is one that has visibly survived challenge

**Application:**
- Show the research process, including its difficulties
- Present the strongest counter-argument before addressing it
- Use moments of personal uncertainty as narrative hooks (the reader identifies with doubt)
- Frame expertise as earned through investigation, not as authority by credential

---

## Theory of Mind and Curiosity

Readers automatically construct models of the author's thinking. They observe the author's reasoning and infer competence, honesty, and reliability.

**In non-fiction, this manifests as:**
- **Subject curiosity:** "What will the author find out?" (drives engagement)
- **Information gaps:** Presenting partial evidence triggers the reader's own hypothesis-building
- **Dramatic irony:** When the reader can see where the evidence is heading before the author states it
- **Method curiosity:** "How did the author get access to this?" "What happened when they tried X?"

**Storr's insight applied:** The brain CANNOT resist trying to fill information gaps. This is the engine of engagement in non-fiction as much as in fiction.

---

## The Flawed Model

Storr: once the brain has constructed its model, it protects it. This applies to conventional wisdom, institutional assumptions, and the reader's own beliefs.

**Application to non-fiction:**
- The book must make the reader's existing model untenable before offering a new one
- Simply presenting correct information is insufficient; the reader must feel why the old model fails
- This is why narrative non-fiction opens with concrete stories that make the problem visceral before introducing systemic analysis
- The reader's "All Is Lost" moment: the chapter where the full scope of the problem becomes undeniable

---

## Summary: The Non-Fiction Research Arc Protocol

When constructing a book's intellectual journey:

1. **Define the initial assumption** -- What does the conventional reader believe?
2. **Establish the author's starting point** -- Where did the research begin?
3. **Set the surface question** -- What question does the reader consciously follow?
4. **Set the deeper pattern** -- What does the evidence actually reveal?
5. **Define the thesis** -- What insight does the journey illuminate?
6. **Map the crisis point** -- Where does the full scope become undeniable?
7. **Choose the arc direction** -- Synthesis (new understanding), tragic (no solution), or advocacy (call to action)?
8. **Design credibility dynamics** -- How does the author earn and maintain the reader's trust?
9. **Plant mystery hooks** -- What will readers want to know at each stage?
10. **Connect to meaning** -- How does the intellectual journey embody the thesis?
