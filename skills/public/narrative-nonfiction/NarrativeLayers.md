# Narrative Layers Architecture

The narrative non-fiction system constructs books across seven simultaneous layers. Think of these as sedimentary strata: each exists independently but they interact, influence, and reinforce each other at every point in the narrative.

These layers are adapted from fiction's seven-layer model. The key difference: in fiction, character transformation drives everything. In non-fiction, the **Intellectual Journey** (Layer 2) is the engine. The author's evolving understanding, and by extension the reader's, is the central arc.

## The Seven Layers

### Layer 1: Meaning (Thesis)

**What it is:** The core argument the book makes about its subject. Every great work of narrative non-fiction has something to say, not merely something to describe.

**How it works:**
- The thesis is NOT stated as a blunt declaration in the opening (that is a textbook, not narrative non-fiction)
- Instead, it emerges from the accumulation of evidence, stories, and analysis
- The thesis should feel inevitable by the final chapters, even if it would have seemed surprising at the start
- Counter-arguments and complications strengthen the thesis by showing it has been tested

**Milestones to map:**
- Thesis introduction (implicit, through the opening scene or question)
- Counter-arguments and complications that deepen the thesis
- Moments where the thesis crystallises through concrete evidence
- Final articulation (through implications, not a summary paragraph)

**Connection to Intellectual Journey:** The thesis is what the author discovered. The Intellectual Journey is how they discovered it. The two are inseparable but distinct.

---

### Layer 2: Intellectual Journey (Research Arc)

**What it is:** The author's evolving understanding of the subject. This replaces fiction's "Character Change" layer. In narrative non-fiction, the author IS the protagonist, and their transformation is one of understanding rather than personality.

**How it works:**
- The author begins with a question or an encounter that does not yet make sense
- Research, interviews, and evidence gradually reshape the author's understanding
- The reader follows the author's path of discovery, experiencing revelations in sequence
- Moments of confusion, surprise, and revised thinking are the narrative's engine

**The Research Arc Engine:**
- **Initial Question (Opening):** What made the author start looking?
- **Early Assumptions:** What did the author believe before the research?
- **Disruptions:** What evidence contradicted the initial assumptions?
- **Revised Understanding:** How did the author's thinking change?
- **Synthesis:** What does the author now understand that they could not have understood at the start?

**Milestones to map (per chapter):**
- What the author knew or believed coming in
- What evidence or encounter changed their thinking
- What the author understands now that they did not before
- How this revised understanding sets up the next chapter's question

**Connection to fiction:** In fiction, the protagonist's "sacred flaw" creates tension between what they want and what they need. In non-fiction, the author's "initial assumption" creates tension between what they expected to find and what the evidence actually shows.

---

### Layer 3: Narrative Thread (Cause and Effect)

**What it is:** The causal chain connecting chapters and sections. Not a sequence of topics, but a chain where each chapter's findings cause the next chapter's question.

**How it works:**
- Every chapter should answer a question raised by the previous chapter
- Every chapter should raise a question that the next chapter must address
- The reader should feel propelled forward, not walked through a syllabus
- "Because of this finding, I had to investigate X" is the connective tissue

**Structural frameworks:**

| Element | Non-Fiction Application |
|---------|----------------------|
| Opening scene | A concrete moment that embodies the book's subject |
| Inciting question | The question the author could not stop thinking about |
| Investigation | The research journey, with its false leads and surprises |
| Complications | Evidence that complicates the emerging picture |
| Crisis point | The moment where the full scope of the problem becomes clear |
| Synthesis | The author's integrated understanding |
| Implications | What should change; what this means for the reader |

**Chapter-level structure:**
Every chapter contains: Opening scene or question, Progressive evidence, Complication or surprise, Revised understanding, Forward hook to next chapter.

---

### Layer 4: Mystery (Information Management)

**What it is:** The systematic control of what the reader knows, suspects, and wonders about at every point. Mystery is NOT just for thriller or crime writing. It is the engine of reader engagement in ALL narrative non-fiction.

**How it works:**
- The brain automatically fills gaps in information
- Presenting partial information triggers hypothesis-building in the reader
- The gap between "what we know" and "what we want to know" is narrative tension
- Confirmation, subversion, or expansion of reader hypotheses creates satisfaction

**Types of mystery to manage in non-fiction:**
- **Primary mystery:** The central question driving the book ("Why do software systems violate human values?" or "How did surveillance capitalism emerge?")
- **Chapter mysteries:** The question each chapter opens with and works to answer
- **Micro-mysteries:** Paragraph-level hooks, unanswered details at section boundaries
- **Subject mysteries:** "Why does this institution behave this way?" "What happened to this person?"
- **Method mysteries:** "How did the author find this out?" (readers enjoy watching the research process)

**Milestones to map:**
- Mystery introduction points (when each question enters the reader's mind)
- Evidence plants (information that will matter in later chapters)
- Misdirections (plausible explanations that later turn out to be incomplete)
- Partial reveals (enough to redirect the reader's thinking, not enough to satisfy)
- Full reveals (resolution of accumulated tension)
- Mystery handoffs (resolving one question while introducing another)

**The "incomplete information" principle:** Never give the reader all the information at once. Every chapter should leave at least one question unanswered.

---

### Layer 5: Landscape (Context and Environment)

**What it is:** The world being described: industry dynamics, institutional forces, social context, historical conditions, regulatory environment. This replaces fiction's "World" layer.

**How it works:**
- Landscape details exist to explain and contextualise the subject, not as background decoration
- Institutional and economic forces create the conditions in which the subject exists
- Historical context shows how we arrived at the present situation
- The landscape should feel alive; it is an active force, not a backdrop

**Key questions to map:**
- What economic incentives drive the behaviour being described?
- What institutional structures enable or constrain the actors?
- What historical developments created the present conditions?
- What regulatory or legal frameworks are relevant?
- How does the landscape differ across geographies or cultures?

**Milestones to map:**
- Landscape establishment (just enough to orient, not an info-dump)
- Progressive landscape revelation (new aspects introduced as the narrative needs them)
- Landscape-as-pressure (how the environment shapes the behaviour of the people in the story)
- Landscape change (how the subject or the research itself is altering the environment)

---

### Layer 6: Relationships (Between People, Ideas, and Institutions)

**What it is:** How the connections between people, ideas, and institutions evolve across the book. In non-fiction, "relationships" encompass more than interpersonal bonds.

**Types of relationship in non-fiction:**
- **Author and subject:** How the author's relationship to their material evolves (from curiosity to concern to advocacy, for example)
- **Between people in the story:** The researchers, the affected users, the corporate actors, the policymakers
- **Between ideas:** How one concept connects to, contradicts, or builds upon another across chapters
- **Between institutions:** How companies, regulators, and advocacy groups interact
- **Author and reader:** The implicit trust relationship; how the author establishes and maintains credibility

**Milestones to map:**
- Relationship establishment (first encounter with a person, idea, or institution)
- Tension points (disagreements, contradictions, competing interests)
- Deepening moments (new evidence that changes the relationship's nature)
- Resolution or transformation (how the relationship stands by the end)

---

### Layer 7: Prose (Style, Rhetoric, Voice)

**What it is:** HOW the material is presented at the sentence level. This is where the rhetorical figures from `RhetoricalFigures.md` are deployed.

**How it works:**
- Prose style is configured per project via Aesthetic Profiles
- Rhetorical figures are deployed strategically at high-impact moments
- The Anti-Cliche system prevents generic, formulaic language
- Voice consistency is maintained through style guidelines

**Key principles:**
- Memorable lines are ENGINEERED, not accidental (Forsyth's thesis)
- Strategic deployment over constant deployment (save the fireworks for key moments)
- Academic evidence and accessible prose are not in conflict; the best non-fiction manages both
- Vary the register: some passages demand precision, others demand lyricism

**See:** `RhetoricalFigures.md` for the full toolbelt
**See:** `AntiCliche.md` for freshness enforcement
**See:** `AestheticProfiles.md` for style configuration

## Layer Interaction Rules

1. **Every chapter advances at least 3 layers.** A chapter that only advances the Narrative Thread is a textbook chapter, not narrative non-fiction.
2. **Intellectual Journey is primary.** All other layers exist to deepen, complicate, or illuminate the author's evolving understanding.
3. **Mystery sustains between chapters.** When evidence is dense, mystery carries the reader's engagement.
4. **Meaning emerges; it does not preach.** The thesis is visible through evidence and narrative, not through declaration.
5. **Landscape serves the argument.** Every contextual detail should eventually matter for the thesis or the people in the story.
6. **Relationships create stakes.** Without human connections (to people, to ideas), the argument remains abstract.
7. **Prose matches moment.** Precise, restrained prose for evidence and analysis; rich, lyrical prose for human stories and emotional peaks.

## Mapping Template

For each major chapter or section, map ALL seven layers:

```
CHAPTER: [Title]

MEANING:              What thesis element is advanced?
INTELLECTUAL JOURNEY: What does the author learn or revise here?
NARRATIVE THREAD:     What question does this chapter answer? What new question does it raise?
MYSTERY:              What information is revealed? What is withheld?
LANDSCAPE:            What context is established or deepened?
RELATIONSHIPS:        Which connections between people, ideas, or institutions are explored?
PROSE:                What register and rhetorical emphasis? What voice?
```

This mapping, done for every chapter, creates the Book Bible.
